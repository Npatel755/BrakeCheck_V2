Collision warning for cars that never got one.

Mount your iPhone on the windshield and BrakeSense keeps track of the car in front of you. If the gap starts closing too fast, it says “Brake” out loud, so you don’t have to look at the screen.

Coming to iPhone. Requires iOS 17 or later.

It stays quiet until the gap closes fast

BrakeSense finds the car in your lane, estimates how far ahead it is, and uses your GPS speed to work out time to collision: how many seconds until you’d reach that car if nothing changed.

The meter at the top of the camera screen climbs through four steps. When it can’t see well enough, it says “No reading” instead of guessing.

  1. Low risk

    A comfortable gap.

  2. Moderate risk

    The gap is shrinking.

  3. High risk

    You’re closing in fast, and the meter grows so it’s hard to miss.

  4. BRAKE NOW

    The edge of the screen flashes red and BrakeSense says “Brake” out loud.

What a drive looks like

Set it up once before you pull out. After that, the warnings and directions come to you.

  1. Before you go

    Mount your phone so the rear camera faces the road, in portrait or landscape. Tap Start trip, and a 10-second check confirms the mount, camera, location and volume.

  2. On the road

    BrakeSense watches the car ahead the whole time. Want directions? Search a place and tap Start navigation. The next turn shows on the camera view and is read out loud.

  3. After you park

    End the trip for a recap: time, top speed, hard brakes and close calls. The last 15 minutes of video stay on your phone, and Save clip keeps a moment for good.

If something hits hard

BrakeSense asks “Are you okay?” and listens for your answer. If you ask for help, or don’t answer within a minute, it messages your emergency contacts with your location. It doesn’t call 911. On iPhone 14 and later, Apple’s Crash Detection can.

A driver aid, not a safety system.

  • Keep your eyes on the road. BrakeSense’s warnings can be late, missed, or wrong.
  • Mount the phone and set it up before you drive. Don’t touch it while moving.
  • Distances and speeds are estimates from the camera and GPS, and are less reliable at night or in rain.

It warns you. It doesn’t brake or steer. It can miss a car in glare, heavy rain, fog or darkness, and it needs a steady mount with a clear view through the windshield.

Your drives stay on your iPhone

Car detection, distance estimates and the spoken check-in all run on the phone’s own chip. Video stays on the phone until you delete it.

The average car on US roads is 12.8 years old,1 and most automakers only made forward-collision warning standard around 2022.2 Cars that have it get into 27% fewer rear-end crashes.3 An iPhone already has the camera and the chip to give an older car that kind of warning, so that’s what we built.

Questions

Which phones does it work on?

iPhones running iOS 17 or later. An Android version is planned.

Does it record video?

While a trip runs, BrakeSense keeps the last 15 minutes of video on your phone and deletes older footage as it goes. Tap Save clip to keep the 15 minutes before and after a moment. A hard jolt saves one automatically. Clips stay on your phone until you delete them.

Will it drain my battery?

The camera, GPS and screen together use a lot of power, and phones get warm on a sunny dashboard. Keep it on a charger and out of direct sun when you can.

Can I mount a phone on my windshield?

It depends on your state. Some allow small mounts in a lower corner of the windshield; others want a dash or vent mount. Check your local rules before you buy one.

What does Premium cost?

Collision warnings, directions, trip recaps and the crash check-in are free. Premium’s price will be shown in the App Store before you buy, and you can cancel any time in your Apple Account settings.
