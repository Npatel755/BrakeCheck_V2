2. A driver aid, not a safety system

BrakeSense estimates distances from a single phone camera. It looks for cars, trucks, buses and motorcycles ahead of you. It isn’t designed to warn you about people, bicycles, animals or objects in the road.

Its warnings can come late, not at all, or when nothing is wrong. That’s most likely in glare, rain, fog or darkness, on hills and curves, through a dirty windshield, or when the phone moves on its mount. BrakeSense only gives warnings. It has no way to brake, steer or control your car.

  • Keep your eyes on the road and drive as if BrakeSense weren’t there.
  • Set up the app and the mount before you drive. Many places ban holding or using a phone while driving, so leave it alone once you’re moving.
  • Mount the phone where the law allows, and where it doesn’t block your view or an airbag.
  • Follow traffic laws, road signs and closures, even when the app’s directions say otherwise. Directions come from Apple Maps and can be wrong or out of date.
  • A Go alert means the car ahead has moved or the light has turned green, and nothing more. Check that the way is clear before you move.

You’re responsible for your driving and for every decision you make while using BrakeSense.

3. Crash check-in and emergency contacts

The check-in depends on your phone, its battery and signal, our server and our messaging provider. Any of them can fail or be slow. BrakeSense can miss an impact, and a pothole or a dropped phone can start a check-in when nothing happened. BrakeSense doesn’t contact 911 or any other emergency service. In an emergency, call 911 or your local emergency number yourself.

Only add people who agree to get messages about you. Each contact has to reply YES before BrakeSense messages them, and can reply STOP at any time. You’re responsible for the names and numbers you add.

The check-in is free. We may change it or stop offering it, and if we stop, we’ll tell you in the app first.
